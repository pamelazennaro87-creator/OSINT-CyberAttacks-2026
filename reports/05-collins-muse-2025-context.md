# Context: Collins Aerospace MUSE Ransomware (September 2025)

Although outside the strict 2026 window, this incident remains the most significant recent public example of operational disruption to European airport passenger processing systems and is frequently referenced in 2026 policy and resilience discussions.

## Summary

- **Date discovered**: 19 September 2025
- **Vendor**: Collins Aerospace (RTX) – MUSE / vMUSE / ARINC cMUSE passenger processing platform
- **Impact**: Automated check-in and boarding systems disrupted at multiple major European airports (Heathrow, Brussels, Berlin, Dublin, Cork and others)
- **Response**: Airports fell back to manual procedures; significant delays and cancellations
- **Attribution / Nature**: ENISA confirmed ransomware. UK NCA made an arrest. Everest Group claimed involvement in some reporting. Dual narratives (data theft vs encryption) existed in open sources.
- **Technical notes from public reporting**: Possible legacy credential / weak FTP exposure vectors discussed in analyses; reinfection during recovery reported in some internal communications relayed by media.

## Why it still matters in 2026

- Demonstrated the systemic risk of shared passenger-processing platforms
- Drove increased European policy attention to aviation cyber resilience (EASA events, national spending increases)
- Serves as the baseline against which 2026 data-only airport incidents are compared
